What air consolidation is.

Your freight flies on a scheduled commercial service, inside a unit built from several importers’ shipments. You pay for the space and weight yours takes up, not for the whole unit. It is the air equivalent of sharing a container.

Several shipments

Yours travels with other importers’ freight heading to the same Australian port.

›

One built unit

Combined onto a single air cargo pallet or container, to use the space properly.

›

One scheduled flight

The unit is uplifted as one consignment. Nobody charters a plane.

›

Split on arrival

Broken down in Australia and delivered as your own consignment, in your name.

Why importers choose it.

It exists to fill the gap between what a courier charges for a pallet and what a direct uplift costs to book.

Cheaper than a courier

Express couriers price small parcels well and larger consignments badly. Once your shipment is a pallet rather than a carton, consolidation is usually the cheaper way to fly it.

Weeks faster than sea

Sea freight from Asia runs in weeks. Consolidation runs in days. When the stock is already late, that gap is the entire decision.

You pay for what you use

No charter, no minimum uplift, no buying space you will not fill. The unit is shared, so the cost of flying it is shared.

One invoice, one team

Pickup, uplift, clearance, terminal charges and delivery on a single written price. We clear it and we cart it, so nothing is handed to a company you have never met.

How chargeable weight works

Length x width x height in centimetres, divided by 6,000, gives the volumetric weight. A carton 80 x 50 x 50cm weighs 10kg on the scales, but the volume calculation gives 33.3kg, so you are charged on 33.3kg. Couriers divide by 5,000 instead, which is why their quote for the same carton looks different.

How it works, step by step.

The sequence is the same on every consolidation. We manage each step, and the ones on Australian soil happen in our own facilities.

01

Collection

Your cartons are collected from your supplier and brought to the origin consolidation warehouse.

02

Sorting

Freight is grouped by destination airport, flight and delivery date, so everything on one unit is going to the same place at the same time.

03

Build

The consignments are combined onto a single air cargo pallet or container to use the space. Sharing the aircraft only saves money if the build is tight, so this is where the saving is actually made.

04

Documentation

One Master Air Waybill covers the whole unit on the flight. Your shipment sits under it on its own House Air Waybill, which is the document that names you as the consignee.

05

Transport

The unit flies to your Australian port as a single consignment on a scheduled commercial service.

06

Deconsolidation

The unit is broken down at the arrival hub and your freight is separated back out against its House Air Waybill.

07

Clearance

Your declaration is lodged before the aircraft lands, so the goods clear instead of collecting storage.

08

Delivery

Your freight leaves on our own truck and is delivered to your door.

How long does air consolidation take from China?

Three to five days airport to airport, with door-to-door timing depending on pickup and clearance.

Is consolidation cheaper than a direct uplift?

For pallet-sized freight it often is, because the unit cost is shared. We compare both options when the shipment is close to the line.

What is the cut-off for a booking?

Cut-offs vary by departure. Send the ready date and we will confirm which consolidation the shipment can make.

How is chargeable weight calculated?

Compare actual weight with volumetric weight calculated from dimensions; the higher figure is charged.
